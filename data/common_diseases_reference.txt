COMMON DISEASES AND CONDITIONS — MEDICAL REFERENCE

RESPIRATORY CONDITIONS

ASTHMA
A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways causing recurrent episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing — especially at night or early morning.
Triggers: allergens (dust mites, pollen, pet dander, mold), respiratory infections, exercise, cold air, smoke, strong odors, stress.
Symptoms: shortness of breath, wheezing (whistling sound when breathing), chest tightness, chronic cough (especially at night).
Diagnosis: spirometry (lung function test), peak flow measurement, allergy testing.
Severity: intermittent, mild persistent, moderate persistent, severe persistent.
Treatment:
- Reliever (rescue) inhalers: Short-acting beta-agonists (salbutamol/albuterol) — for immediate relief during attacks.
- Controller (preventer) inhalers: Inhaled corticosteroids (beclomethasone, fluticasone, budesonide) — taken daily to prevent attacks.
- Long-acting beta-agonists (salmeterol): Added to ICS for moderate-severe asthma.
- Montelukast (leukotriene modifier): Oral tablet, especially useful for allergic asthma.
- Oral corticosteroids: Short courses for severe attacks.
Asthma attack emergency signs: Blue lips, inability to speak full sentences, not responding to reliever inhaler — call 108/911 immediately.

CHRONIC OBSTRUCTIVE PULMONARY DISEASE (COPD)
Progressive lung disease including chronic bronchitis and emphysema. Main cause: cigarette smoking (80–90% of cases). Also caused by long-term exposure to dust, chemical fumes, air pollution.
Symptoms: gradual worsening breathlessness, chronic cough with mucus, frequent respiratory infections, wheezing, fatigue, unintentional weight loss (advanced).
Diagnosis: Spirometry (FEV1/FVC ratio below 0.70).
Treatment: Stop smoking (most important intervention), bronchodilators (tiotropium, salbutamol), ICS, pulmonary rehabilitation, oxygen therapy (severe), vaccinations (flu, pneumonia).

PNEUMONIA
Infection that inflames the air sacs (alveoli) in the lung. Can be bacterial, viral, fungal, or aspiration.
Symptoms: cough with phlegm, fever, chills, difficulty breathing, chest pain with breathing, fatigue, nausea/vomiting.
Bacterial pneumonia (most common serious type): caused by Streptococcus pneumoniae. Treated with antibiotics.
Community-acquired pneumonia: amoxicillin or amoxicillin-clavulanate, azithromycin (in India).
Hospitalization required if: oxygen saturation below 92%, very high fever, multi-lobar involvement, elderly or immunocompromised patients.

TUBERCULOSIS (TB)
Bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Major public health issue in India (India has highest TB burden globally).
Pulmonary TB symptoms: persistent cough for 2+ weeks, blood in sputum, night sweats, weight loss, fever, fatigue.
Diagnosis: sputum smear microscopy, GeneXpert (CBNAAT), chest X-ray, tuberculin skin test (Mantoux).
Treatment: DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment, Short-Course) — 6 months minimum. First-line drugs: Isoniazid (H), Rifampicin (R), Pyrazinamide (Z), Ethambutol (E). Never stop early — leads to drug-resistant TB.
Drug-Resistant TB (MDR-TB): Resistant to at least Isoniazid and Rifampicin. Requires longer treatment (18–24 months) with second-line drugs.

THYROID DISORDERS

HYPOTHYROIDISM
Underactive thyroid — not enough thyroid hormone production.
Causes: Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune, most common), thyroid surgery, radioiodine treatment, iodine deficiency.
Symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, constipation, hair loss, depression, slow heart rate, muscle weakness, irregular periods.
Diagnosis: TSH elevated, T3/T4 low.
Treatment: Levothyroxine (synthetic T4) — taken daily on empty stomach, 30 minutes before food. Dose adjusted based on TSH levels.

HYPERTHYROIDISM
Overactive thyroid — too much thyroid hormone.
Causes: Graves' disease (autoimmune — most common), toxic multinodular goiter, thyroid adenoma.
Symptoms: weight loss despite increased appetite, rapid/irregular heartbeat, heat intolerance, excessive sweating, tremors, anxiety, irritability, frequent bowel movements, fatigue, prominent eyes (Graves' disease).
Diagnosis: TSH low, T3/T4 elevated.
Treatment: Anti-thyroid drugs (carbimazole, propylthiouracil), radioiodine therapy, thyroid surgery (thyroidectomy).

ARTHRITIS

OSTEOARTHRITIS (OA)
"Wear and tear" arthritis — most common form. Cartilage in joints breaks down.
Most commonly affects: knees, hips, spine, hands.
Symptoms: joint pain and stiffness (worse in morning or after inactivity), tenderness, reduced range of motion, grating sensation, bone spurs.
Risk factors: older age, obesity, previous joint injury, repetitive stress, family history, female sex.
Treatment: Exercise (low-impact: swimming, cycling), weight loss (critical for knee OA), physical therapy, paracetamol and NSAIDs for pain, topical NSAIDs, intra-articular corticosteroid injections, joint replacement surgery (severe cases).

RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS (RA)
Autoimmune form of arthritis — immune system attacks joint lining (synovium).
Typically affects: small joints of hands and feet, wrists, knees, ankles. Usually symmetric (both sides).
Symptoms: joints that are swollen, warm, tender, stiff (especially morning stiffness lasting >1 hour), fatigue, fever, weight loss. Can affect organs (lungs, heart, eyes, kidneys).
Diagnosis: rheumatoid factor (RF), anti-CCP antibodies, CRP, ESR, X-rays.
Treatment: DMARDs — Disease-Modifying Anti-Rheumatic Drugs. Methotrexate (first-line). Hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, leflunomide. Biologics (TNF inhibitors: adalimumab, etanercept) for moderate-severe RA. NSAIDs and corticosteroids for symptom control.

KIDNEY (RENAL) DISEASE

CHRONIC KIDNEY DISEASE (CKD)
Gradual loss of kidney function over months to years. Most common causes: diabetes mellitus (type 1 and 2), hypertension. Other causes: glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, recurrent kidney infections.
Stages: 1–5 based on GFR (glomerular filtration rate). Stage 5 = kidney failure (GFR <15 mL/min/1.73m²) requiring dialysis or transplant.
Symptoms (often no symptoms until advanced): fatigue, fluid retention (swelling in legs, ankles, feet), shortness of breath, appetite loss, nausea, confusion, blood in urine, foamy urine, hypertension.
Key blood tests: serum creatinine, eGFR, urea. Urine: protein-to-creatinine ratio.
Treatment: Control blood pressure (target <130/80), control blood sugar (diabetics), ACE inhibitors/ARBs (protect kidneys), low-protein diet, avoid nephrotoxic drugs (NSAIDs, contrast dye), treat anemia with erythropoietin, dialysis (hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis) for end-stage renal disease.

KIDNEY STONES (NEPHROLITHIASIS)
Hard deposits from minerals and salts forming in the kidneys.
Types: calcium oxalate (most common), calcium phosphate, uric acid, struvite, cystine.
Symptoms: severe flank pain (renal colic) coming in waves, pain radiating to lower abdomen and groin, blood in urine (hematuria), painful urination, nausea, vomiting, frequent urination, fever/chills (if infected).
Treatment: Small stones (<5mm): pass on own with hydration (2–3L water/day) and pain relief (NSAIDs, opioids). Tamsulosin helps passage. Larger stones or blocked stones: lithotripsy (ESWL — shock waves), ureteroscopy, or percutaneous nephrolithotomy. Prevention: high fluid intake, dietary modifications based on stone type.

URINARY TRACT INFECTION (UTI)
Infection in any part of the urinary system (kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra). More common in women (shorter urethra).
Types: Cystitis (bladder infection — most common), pyelonephritis (kidney infection — more serious).
Symptoms: burning sensation on urination, frequent urge to urinate, passing small amounts of urine, cloudy or strong-smelling urine, pelvic pain (women), blood in urine. Kidney infection adds: fever, back/flank pain, nausea, vomiting.
Treatment: Antibiotics — trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, nitrofurantoin, or ciprofloxacin. Drink plenty of water. Pyelonephritis may require IV antibiotics and hospitalization. Always complete full course.

LIVER DISEASE

HEPATITIS B AND C
Viral infections of the liver. Hepatitis B: transmitted through blood, sexual contact, mother-to-child. Hepatitis C: primarily through blood (IV drug use, unsterilized needles). Both can cause chronic infection, cirrhosis, liver failure, liver cancer.
Hepatitis B prevention: Vaccination (highly effective). Hepatitis C: No vaccine; treat with direct-acting antivirals (DAAs) — highly effective (95%+ cure rate).
Symptoms: Often no symptoms for years (silent). When present: fatigue, jaundice (yellow skin/eyes), dark urine, abdominal pain, nausea, loss of appetite.

FATTY LIVER DISEASE (NAFLD / MASLD)
Accumulation of fat in the liver not caused by alcohol. NAFLD = Non-Alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease (now renamed MASLD). Strongly associated with obesity, type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, high cholesterol.
Symptoms: Usually no symptoms. May cause fatigue, upper right abdominal discomfort.
Diagnosis: Ultrasound, elevated liver enzymes (ALT, AST).
Treatment: Weight loss (5–10% reduction significantly improves liver health), exercise, healthy diet, control blood sugar and cholesterol. No approved medications yet (research ongoing).

HEART CONDITIONS

CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE (CAD)
Plaque buildup in coronary arteries, reducing blood flow to the heart.
Risk factors: High LDL cholesterol, hypertension, smoking, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, family history, age, male sex.
Symptoms: Angina (chest pain/pressure with exertion or stress), shortness of breath, palpitations.
Complications: Heart attack (myocardial infarction), heart failure, arrhythmia, sudden cardiac death.
Treatment: Lifestyle modification, medications (aspirin, statins, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, nitrates), coronary angioplasty (PCI), coronary artery bypass surgery (CABG).

HEART FAILURE
The heart cannot pump enough blood to meet the body's needs. Does NOT mean the heart stops — it means it works less efficiently.
Types: HFrEF (reduced ejection fraction — weak pump), HFpEF (preserved ejection fraction — stiff heart).
Common causes: CAD, hypertension, diabetes, cardiomyopathy, valvular heart disease.
Symptoms: Breathlessness (especially lying flat), leg/ankle swelling, fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, rapid or irregular heartbeat, cough (sometimes with pink frothy sputum).
Treatment: ACE inhibitors/ARBs, beta-blockers, diuretics (furosemide), spironolactone, SGLT2 inhibitors (now proven beneficial in heart failure), ICD/CRT devices in selected patients, heart transplant (end-stage).

CANCER — KEY FACTS

Common symptoms that may indicate cancer (see a doctor promptly):
- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent fatigue
- A lump that does not go away
- Unexplained bleeding or bruising
- Changes in bowel or bladder habits
- Persistent cough or hoarseness
- Changes in a mole or skin lesion
- Difficulty swallowing

Note on cancer: Cancer is not one disease — it encompasses hundreds of different conditions. Most cancers are more treatable when caught early. Regular screening (mammogram, Pap smear, colonoscopy) is the most effective prevention tool.
