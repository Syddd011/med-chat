DIABETES MELLITUS — COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL REFERENCE

DEFINITION
Diabetes mellitus is a group of metabolic diseases characterized by high blood sugar (glucose) resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both. Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that allows blood sugar to enter cells to be used for energy.

TYPES OF DIABETES

TYPE 1 DIABETES
An autoimmune condition where the immune system attacks and destroys insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. The body makes little to no insulin. It can develop at any age but usually appears during childhood or adolescence. People with type 1 diabetes must take insulin every day to survive. Symptoms typically develop quickly. Symptoms include: frequent urination, excessive thirst, unexplained weight loss, extreme hunger, blurred vision, fatigue, and mood changes.

TYPE 2 DIABETES
The most common form of diabetes. The body does not use insulin well (insulin resistance) and cannot keep blood sugar at normal levels. It develops over many years and is usually diagnosed in adults over 45, but increasingly in children, teens, and young adults. Many people have no symptoms. It is usually managed with lifestyle changes, oral medications, and sometimes insulin.

GESTATIONAL DIABETES
Develops during pregnancy in women who do not already have diabetes. It usually goes away after the baby is born, but it increases the risk of type 2 diabetes later. It can cause health problems for both mother and baby.

PREDIABETES
Blood sugar levels are higher than normal but not yet high enough to be diagnosed as type 2 diabetes. Without lifestyle changes, prediabetes often progresses to type 2 diabetes.

BLOOD SUGAR TARGETS (GENERAL GUIDELINES)
- Normal fasting blood glucose: 70-99 mg/dL
- Prediabetes fasting: 100-125 mg/dL
- Diabetes fasting: 126 mg/dL or higher on two separate tests
- HbA1c (measures 3-month average): Less than 5.7% normal, 5.7-6.4% prediabetes, 6.5% or higher is diabetes

SYMPTOMS OF DIABETES
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Extreme hunger (polyphagia)
- Blurred vision
- Fatigue and lack of energy
- Slow healing of wounds or frequent infections
- Tingling, numbness, or pain in hands or feet
- Patches of dark skin in body creases

COMPLICATIONS
Short-term: Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) — shakiness, sweating, confusion, loss of consciousness. Hyperglycemia (high blood sugar) — frequent urination, increased thirst, blurred vision. Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — life-threatening emergency mainly in type 1.

Long-term: Heart disease and stroke, nerve damage (neuropathy), kidney damage (nephropathy), eye damage (retinopathy and blindness), foot damage, skin and mouth conditions, depression and anxiety, Alzheimer's disease.

DIAGNOSIS
- Fasting blood glucose test
- HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin) test
- Random plasma glucose test
- Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)

TREATMENT

Type 1 Diabetes:
- Insulin therapy (multiple daily injections or insulin pump)
- Blood sugar monitoring (finger-stick or continuous glucose monitor)
- Carbohydrate counting
- Regular physical activity
- Healthy eating

Type 2 Diabetes:
- Lifestyle changes: healthy eating, physical activity, weight loss
- Oral medications: Metformin (first-line), sulfonylureas (glipizide, glimepiride), SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin), GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide, liraglutide), DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin)
- Insulin therapy if needed
- Blood pressure and cholesterol management

DIET FOR DIABETES
- Choose foods with a low glycemic index
- Eat plenty of non-starchy vegetables
- Include lean proteins
- Choose whole grains over refined grains
- Limit sugary drinks, sweets, and processed foods
- Control portion sizes
- Do not skip meals

HYPOGLYCEMIA (LOW BLOOD SUGAR) - EMERGENCY
Symptoms: shakiness, sweating, rapid heartbeat, hunger, dizziness, nausea, blurred vision, headache, confusion, irritability, pale skin.
Treatment (Rule of 15): Eat 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrates (glucose tablets, fruit juice, regular soda, candy). Wait 15 minutes. Recheck blood sugar. Repeat if still low. Seek emergency help if unconscious.

PREVENTION OF TYPE 2 DIABETES
- Maintain a healthy weight
- Exercise regularly (at least 150 minutes per week)
- Eat a healthy diet with less sugar and refined carbs
- Quit smoking
- Limit alcohol
- Get regular blood sugar screenings

WHEN TO SEE A DOCTOR
See a doctor if you have symptoms of diabetes, if you are at high risk (overweight, family history, prediabetes), or if you have blood sugar readings outside normal range. Diabetes is a serious but manageable condition with proper medical care.
