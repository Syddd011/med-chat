LIFESTYLE AND WELLNESS — COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL REFERENCE

PHYSICAL ACTIVITY AND EXERCISE

RECOMMENDED PHYSICAL ACTIVITY GUIDELINES (WHO / ICMR)
Adults (18–64 years):
- At least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week (e.g. brisk walking, cycling, swimming), OR
- At least 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity per week (e.g. running, fast cycling, aerobics), OR
- An equivalent combination of both.
- Muscle-strengthening activities (weight training, bodyweight exercises, yoga): 2 or more days per week.

Older adults (65+): Same as adults, plus balance and fall-prevention exercises (e.g. Tai Chi, single-leg stand) at least 3 days per week.

Children (5–17 years): At least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity per day.

TYPES OF EXERCISE
Aerobic (cardiovascular) exercise: Increases heart rate and breathing. Burns calories, improves heart and lung health, reduces blood pressure, improves blood sugar control. Examples: walking, jogging, cycling, swimming, dancing, Zumba.
Strength/Resistance training: Builds and maintains muscle mass, increases bone density, improves metabolism. Examples: weight training, resistance bands, push-ups, squats, yoga, Pilates.
Flexibility and mobility: Improves range of motion, reduces injury risk. Examples: stretching, yoga, Tai Chi.
Balance training: Reduces fall risk, especially in older adults. Examples: standing on one leg, heel-to-toe walking, Tai Chi.

EXERCISE AND DISEASE PREVENTION
Heart disease: Regular aerobic exercise lowers LDL cholesterol, raises HDL cholesterol, reduces blood pressure, lowers heart attack risk by 30–50%.
Type 2 diabetes: Exercise improves insulin sensitivity and blood sugar control. Even 30 minutes of walking 5 days/week significantly reduces risk.
Cancer: Physical activity is associated with lower risk of colon, breast, endometrial, and several other cancers.
Mental health: Exercise increases serotonin, dopamine, and endorphins — as effective as antidepressants for mild-moderate depression. Reduces anxiety and stress.
Osteoporosis: Weight-bearing exercise (walking, dancing, weight training) maintains and builds bone density.
Obesity: Exercise combined with diet change is the most effective weight management strategy.

STARTING EXERCISE SAFELY
See a doctor before starting if you have heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, severe joint problems, or any chronic illness. Start slowly and increase gradually (10% rule: don't increase intensity by more than 10% per week). Warm up before and cool down after exercise. Stay hydrated. Listen to your body — stop if you feel chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or faintness.

SLEEP HEALTH

SLEEP RECOMMENDATIONS
Adults: 7–9 hours per night. Less than 6 hours or more than 9 hours consistently linked to health problems.

SLEEP STAGES
NREM Stage 1: Light sleep — transition into sleep. NREM Stage 2: Core sleep — body temperature drops, heart rate slows. NREM Stage 3 (Deep Sleep): Most restorative — tissue repair, immune strengthening, memory consolidation. REM Sleep: Dreaming — emotional processing, learning, memory.

CONSEQUENCES OF POOR SLEEP
Cognitive: impaired concentration, poor memory, slow reaction time, increased risk of accidents. Physical: increased risk of obesity, type 2 diabetes, heart disease, hypertension, weakened immune system, chronic pain. Mental: worsens depression and anxiety. Hormonal: disrupts cortisol, insulin, leptin (hunger hormone), ghrelin.

SLEEP HYGIENE (TIPS FOR BETTER SLEEP)
Keep a consistent sleep schedule — same bedtime and wake time, even on weekends. Create a dark, quiet, cool sleep environment (18–20°C / 65–68°F is ideal). Avoid screens (phones, tablets, TVs) for at least 1 hour before bed — blue light suppresses melatonin. Avoid caffeine after 2pm. Avoid heavy meals within 2–3 hours of bedtime. Avoid alcohol close to bedtime — disrupts sleep quality even if it helps initiation. Exercise regularly but not within 2–3 hours of bedtime. Wind down with relaxing activities (reading, warm bath, meditation, gentle stretching). If you can't sleep, don't lie in bed awake — get up and do something calm until sleepy.

INSOMNIA
Difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, despite adequate opportunity. Chronic insomnia: occurring at least 3 nights/week for 3+ months.
Treatment: CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia) — most effective long-term treatment. Sleep restriction therapy, stimulus control, relaxation training. Medications: Short-term use only — zolpidem, zopiclone, melatonin, low-dose doxepin.

STRESS MANAGEMENT

WHAT IS STRESS
Stress is the body's response to demands or threats. Short-term (acute) stress can be helpful (fight-or-flight). Chronic (long-term) stress damages health.

EFFECTS OF CHRONIC STRESS
Cardiovascular: increased blood pressure, heart disease risk. Immune: weakened immune response — more vulnerable to infections. Digestive: IBS, acid reflux, stomach ulcers. Mental: anxiety, depression, burnout. Sleep: insomnia, poor sleep quality. Endocrine: disrupts cortisol and other hormones. Musculoskeletal: tension headaches, back/neck pain.

STRESS MANAGEMENT TECHNIQUES
Physical: Regular exercise (most effective), adequate sleep, healthy nutrition, limit alcohol and caffeine.
Mindfulness and meditation: 10–20 minutes daily significantly reduces stress and anxiety. Apps: Headspace, Calm, Insight Timer.
Deep breathing: Slows heart rate and activates parasympathetic nervous system. 4-7-8 breathing: Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 7, exhale slowly for 8.
Progressive muscle relaxation: Tense and release muscle groups sequentially.
Time management: Prioritize, delegate, break tasks into smaller steps. Learn to say no.
Social connection: Strong social relationships are one of the most protective factors for mental health and longevity.
Journaling: Writing about stress helps process emotions and gain perspective.
Therapy: CBT is highly effective for chronic stress, anxiety, and depression.
Hobbies and leisure: Make time for activities you enjoy.
Nature: Even 20 minutes in a park or green space measurably reduces cortisol.

SMOKING CESSATION

HEALTH EFFECTS OF SMOKING
Smoking is the leading preventable cause of death globally. Damages nearly every organ. Causes: lung cancer (responsible for 85% of cases), COPD, emphysema, heart disease, stroke, peripheral arterial disease, type 2 diabetes, oral cancer, esophageal cancer, bladder cancer, cervical cancer, kidney cancer, pancreatic cancer, reproductive issues, premature aging, poor wound healing.

BENEFITS OF QUITTING
20 minutes: Heart rate and blood pressure drop. 12 hours: Carbon monoxide levels normalize. 2–12 weeks: Circulation improves, lung function increases. 1–9 months: Coughs and shortness of breath decrease. 1 year: Risk of heart disease halved. 5 years: Stroke risk reduces to that of a non-smoker. 10 years: Lung cancer risk halved. 15 years: Heart disease risk same as non-smoker.

HOW TO QUIT
Behavioral: Set a quit date. Tell family and friends. Remove cigarettes, lighters, ashtrays. Identify and plan for triggers. Seek support — group counseling is highly effective.
Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT): Patches, gum, lozenges, inhaler, nasal spray. Reduces withdrawal symptoms.
Medications: Varenicline (Champix/Chantix) — most effective pharmacotherapy. Bupropion — antidepressant that reduces cravings. Use in combination with behavioral support.
Apps and support: iCan Quit, Smoke Free, QuitNow. In India: National Tobacco Quitline 1800-11-2356.

ALCOHOL AND HEALTH

SAFE LIMITS (GENERAL GUIDELINES)
Men: No more than 14 units per week (UK), spread over 3+ days. Women: No more than 7–10 units/week. 1 unit = 10ml pure alcohol = 1 small glass of wine / half pint of beer / 1 measure of spirits.
No amount of alcohol is completely safe. The less, the better for health.

HEALTH EFFECTS OF EXCESSIVE ALCOHOL
Liver: fatty liver, alcoholic hepatitis, cirrhosis, liver cancer. Cancer: oral, esophageal, liver, colorectal, breast. Cardiovascular: cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias. Brain: dementia, Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome (thiamine deficiency). Mental health: depression, anxiety, dependency, psychosis. Immune system: impaired — increased susceptibility to infections. Social: relationship problems, domestic violence, accidents, job loss.

WEIGHT MANAGEMENT

HEALTHY WEIGHT PRINCIPLES
Sustainable weight loss = 0.5–1 kg per week (500–1000 calorie daily deficit). Crash diets lead to muscle loss and regain. Focus on lifestyle change, not short-term diet.
Practical strategies: Eat slowly (takes 20 minutes for fullness signals to reach brain). Use smaller plates. Eat high-fiber, high-protein foods (more filling). Avoid liquid calories (juice, soda, alcohol). Read food labels. Cook at home more. Plan meals. Don't skip meals (leads to overeating later). Get adequate sleep (sleep deprivation increases appetite and cravings).

ORAL HEALTH AND DENTAL CARE
Brush teeth twice daily with fluoride toothpaste. Floss daily. Use mouthwash. Visit dentist every 6 months. Limit sugary and acidic foods. Do not smoke (causes gum disease and oral cancer). Drink water (fluoridated where available). Replace toothbrush every 3–4 months.

Signs of dental problems requiring prompt care: Toothache, swollen gums, bleeding gums, loose teeth, sensitivity to hot/cold, bad breath despite good hygiene, mouth sores that don't heal in 2 weeks, changes in color or lumps in mouth (see a dentist — could be oral cancer).
