WOMEN'S HEALTH — COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL REFERENCE

MENSTRUAL HEALTH

NORMAL MENSTRUAL CYCLE
A normal menstrual cycle is 21–35 days long, with menstruation (period) lasting 2–7 days. Average blood loss is 30–80 mL. The cycle is regulated by hormones: estrogen, progesterone, LH (luteinizing hormone), and FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone).

DYSMENORRHEA (PAINFUL PERIODS)
Primary dysmenorrhea: Cramping pain before/during periods with no underlying condition. Caused by prostaglandins that cause uterine contractions.
Secondary dysmenorrhea: Pain caused by an underlying condition (endometriosis, fibroids, PID).
Treatment: NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) — most effective when started 1–2 days before period. Heat therapy. Oral contraceptives. Exercise. Omega-3 supplementation.

MENORRHAGIA (HEAVY PERIODS)
Soaking more than one pad/tampon per hour for several consecutive hours, or periods lasting more than 7 days.
Causes: fibroids, polyps, hormonal imbalance, thyroid disorders, bleeding disorders, IUDs.
Treatment: NSAIDs, tranexamic acid, hormonal contraceptives, iron supplementation for anemia. Surgical options for severe cases.

AMENORRHEA (ABSENT PERIODS)
Primary: menstruation has never occurred by age 15.
Secondary: periods stop for 3+ months in a woman who previously had regular cycles.
Causes: pregnancy (most common), extreme weight loss, excessive exercise, stress, PCOS, thyroid disorders, premature ovarian insufficiency, contraceptives.

PREMENSTRUAL SYNDROME (PMS)
Emotional and physical symptoms 1–2 weeks before period: mood swings, irritability, bloating, breast tenderness, fatigue, food cravings, depression.
PMDD (Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder): Severe PMS with significant mood symptoms. Treated with SSRIs, hormonal contraceptives, lifestyle changes.

POLYCYSTIC OVARY SYNDROME (PCOS)
A hormonal disorder with multiple small cysts on the ovaries. One of the most common hormonal disorders in women of reproductive age.
Symptoms: irregular or absent periods, excess androgen (acne, hirsutism, male-pattern hair thinning), enlarged ovaries with cysts, weight gain, difficulty losing weight, darkened skin patches (acanthosis nigricans), infertility.
Diagnosis: Rotterdam criteria (2 of 3): irregular ovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries on ultrasound.
Complications: infertility, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, endometrial cancer risk, depression.
Treatment: lifestyle modification (weight loss of 5–10% significantly improves symptoms), oral contraceptives (regulate periods, reduce androgens), metformin (for insulin resistance), clomiphene (for fertility), spironolactone (for androgen symptoms).

ENDOMETRIOSIS
The lining of the uterus (endometrium) grows outside the uterus — on ovaries, fallopian tubes, bowel, or bladder.
Symptoms: severe pelvic pain (especially during periods), pain during intercourse, pain with bowel movements or urination, heavy periods, infertility, fatigue.
Diagnosis: laparoscopy (gold standard), ultrasound can detect endometriomas (ovarian cysts).
Treatment: pain medications (NSAIDs), hormonal therapies (contraceptives, GnRH agonists, progestins), laparoscopic surgery, in severe cases hysterectomy.

UTERINE FIBROIDS (LEIOMYOMAS)
Non-cancerous growths in or on the uterus. Very common — up to 70–80% of women develop fibroids by age 50.
Symptoms (many have no symptoms): heavy or prolonged periods, pelvic pressure or pain, frequent urination, constipation, backache, leg pain, difficulty getting pregnant.
Treatment: watchful waiting if no symptoms, medications (GnRH agonists to shrink fibroids), non-surgical procedures (uterine fibroid embolization), surgical options (myomectomy to remove fibroids, hysterectomy).

PREGNANCY

SIGNS OF PREGNANCY
Missed period, positive pregnancy test, nausea/vomiting (morning sickness), breast tenderness, frequent urination, fatigue, light spotting (implantation bleeding), mild cramping, food aversions, heightened sense of smell, mood changes.

HOW PREGNANCY IS CONFIRMED
Home urine pregnancy test detects hCG hormone (most accurate from first day of missed period). Blood test (quantitative hCG) confirms pregnancy and can detect earlier. Ultrasound confirms gestational age and location.

TRIMESTERS OF PREGNANCY
First trimester (weeks 1–12): Embryo develops all major organs. Morning sickness common. Risk of miscarriage highest. Avoid alcohol, smoking, raw meat, unpasteurized foods, high-mercury fish.
Second trimester (weeks 13–26): "Honeymoon trimester" — nausea usually improves. Baby movements felt (quickening). Anatomy ultrasound at 18–20 weeks. Gestational diabetes screening at 24–28 weeks.
Third trimester (weeks 27–40): Baby grows rapidly. Shortness of breath, back pain, Braxton Hicks contractions. Prepare for delivery.

PRENATAL CARE ESSENTIALS
Folic acid: 400–800 mcg before conception and in first trimester (prevents neural tube defects).
Iron: 27 mg/day (prevents anemia).
Calcium: 1000 mg/day.
DHA/Omega-3: for baby's brain and eye development.
Vitamin D: 600 IU/day.
Avoid: alcohol (no safe amount), smoking, raw/undercooked meat, raw eggs, unpasteurized dairy, high-mercury fish, excessive caffeine (limit to 200mg/day), certain medications.

COMMON PREGNANCY COMPLICATIONS
Gestational diabetes: High blood sugar during pregnancy. Managed with diet, exercise, and sometimes insulin. Resolves after delivery but increases type 2 diabetes risk.
Preeclampsia: High blood pressure + protein in urine after 20 weeks. Symptoms: severe headache, visual changes, upper abdominal pain, severe swelling. Requires immediate medical attention.
Miscarriage: Loss of pregnancy before 20 weeks. Most common in first trimester. Symptoms: vaginal bleeding, cramping, tissue passing. Seek medical care immediately.
Ectopic pregnancy: Pregnancy implants outside uterus (usually fallopian tube). Symptoms: sharp pelvic pain, bleeding, shoulder pain. Medical emergency requiring immediate treatment.

POSTPARTUM

POSTPARTUM DEPRESSION (PPD)
Affects 10–15% of new mothers. Different from "baby blues" (mild sadness lasting 1–2 weeks post-delivery).
Symptoms: persistent sadness, crying, difficulty bonding with baby, withdrawal from family, changes in appetite and sleep, feeling worthless or hopeless, severe anxiety, thoughts of harming self or baby.
Treatment: psychotherapy (CBT), SSRIs (safe with breastfeeding), support groups. Seek help immediately if thoughts of harm to self or baby.

BREASTFEEDING
Benefits for baby: optimal nutrition, immunity boost (through antibodies), reduced risk of infections, SIDS, obesity.
Benefits for mother: uterine contraction, reduced postpartum bleeding, lower risk of breast/ovarian cancer, bonding, caloric burn.
WHO recommends exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months, continued with solid foods up to 2 years.

MENOPAUSE

DEFINITION
Natural cessation of menstruation for 12 consecutive months. Average age in India: 46–48 years. Average age globally: 51 years.

PERIMENOPAUSE
The transition phase before menopause (can last 4–10 years). Irregular periods, early menopause symptoms begin.

SYMPTOMS
Hot flashes and night sweats, vaginal dryness and discomfort during sex, sleep disturbances, mood changes (irritability, anxiety, depression), memory and concentration changes, weight gain (especially around abdomen), thinning hair, dry skin, decreased libido, urinary frequency and urgency.

HEALTH RISKS AFTER MENOPAUSE
Osteoporosis (bone density loss — estrogen protects bones), heart disease risk increases, cognitive changes.

MANAGEMENT
Lifestyle: regular exercise (weight-bearing and aerobic), healthy diet (calcium and vitamin D), not smoking, limiting alcohol.
Hormone replacement therapy (HRT/MHT): Effective for vasomotor symptoms (hot flashes). Estrogen-only (for women without uterus) or combined estrogen+progestogen. Discuss risks vs. benefits with a doctor.
Non-hormonal: SSRIs/SNRIs for hot flashes, vaginal lubricants and moisturizers, cognitive behavioral therapy for sleep and mood.

CERVICAL CANCER SCREENING (PAP SMEAR)
Pap smear (Pap test) detects abnormal cervical cells caused by HPV (human papillomavirus).
Recommended screening: Every 3 years from age 21–65 (Pap alone), or every 5 years from age 30 with HPV co-test.
HPV Vaccine: Protects against cancer-causing HPV strains. Most effective when given before sexual activity begins. Recommended for girls and boys aged 9–14 in India.

BREAST HEALTH
Monthly self-breast exam: Check for lumps, skin changes (dimpling, redness), nipple discharge or inversion, change in size or shape.
Signs to report to doctor immediately: New lump or firmness, skin changes, nipple discharge (especially bloody), nipple inversion.
Mammogram screening: Recommended annually from age 40–74 (varies by guideline). Earlier if high risk (family history, BRCA gene).

SEXUALLY TRANSMITTED INFECTIONS (STIs) IN WOMEN
Common: chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis, herpes (HSV), HPV, trichomonas, HIV.
Women often have no symptoms (especially chlamydia and gonorrhea). If untreated, can cause pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), infertility, chronic pelvic pain.
Prevention: Correct condom use, regular screening, HPV vaccination, limiting partners.

VAGINAL HEALTH
Normal vaginal discharge: Clear to white, mild odor, amount varies with cycle. Changes in color (green/grey), odor (fishy), or texture warrant evaluation.
Bacterial vaginosis (BV): Most common vaginal infection. Fishy odor, grey-white discharge. Not an STI but associated with sexual activity. Treated with metronidazole or clindamycin.
Vulvovaginal candidiasis (yeast infection): White cottage-cheese discharge, intense itching, redness. Treated with antifungal (fluconazole tablet or clotrimazole cream).
