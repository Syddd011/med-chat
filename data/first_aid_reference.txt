FIRST AID — ESSENTIAL GUIDE FOR COMMON EMERGENCIES

GENERAL PRINCIPLES
Always call emergency services first in a serious emergency. In India call 108 (ambulance), 100 (police). In USA call 911. In UK call 999. Globally call 112.
Do not move an injured person unless they are in immediate danger.
Stay calm, reassure the victim, and keep them still and warm.

CPR (CARDIOPULMONARY RESUSCITATION)

WHEN TO USE CPR
Use CPR when a person is not breathing and has no pulse. Only trained individuals should perform CPR. Bystander CPR significantly increases survival chances.

ADULT CPR STEPS (Hands-only if not trained)
1. Check that the scene is safe.
2. Tap the person's shoulder firmly and shout: "Are you okay?"
3. Call 108 (India) or 911 (USA) immediately, or ask someone nearby to call.
4. Place the person on their back on a firm, flat surface.
5. Kneel beside them at chest level.
6. Place the heel of one hand on the center of the chest (on the breastbone).
7. Place the other hand on top and interlace your fingers.
8. Keep your arms straight and push down hard and fast — compress at least 2 inches deep.
9. Allow the chest to fully recoil between compressions.
10. Push at a rate of 100-120 compressions per minute (to the beat of "Stayin' Alive").
11. If trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions (30:2 ratio).
12. Continue until help arrives, the person shows signs of life, an AED is available, or you are too exhausted.

CHOKING — HEIMLICH MANEUVER (ADULTS)
Signs: Cannot speak, cough, or breathe; clutching throat.
1. Ask: "Are you choking?" If they cannot speak, act immediately.
2. Call 108 or 911.
3. Stand behind the person with one foot forward for balance.
4. Lean them slightly forward.
5. Make a fist with one hand and place the thumb side against the middle of the abdomen, just above the navel and well below the breastbone.
6. Grasp your fist with the other hand and give quick, upward abdominal thrusts.
7. Repeat until the object is expelled or the person becomes unconscious.
If unconscious: begin CPR. Each time you open the airway to give a breath, look for the object and remove it only if you can see it.

CHOKING — INFANT (Under 1 year)
1. Hold the baby face-down on your forearm, supporting the head.
2. Give 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. Turn the baby face-up and give 5 chest thrusts using 2 fingers on the center of the chest.
4. Repeat until the object is dislodged or the infant loses consciousness.

BLEEDING — SEVERE
1. Apply firm, direct pressure using a clean cloth or sterile bandage.
2. Do not remove the cloth. If it soaks through, add more on top.
3. Elevate the injured limb above the heart if possible.
4. Maintain pressure for at least 10-15 minutes without peeking.
5. If bleeding does not stop or is life-threatening, call 108/911 immediately.
6. For severe limb bleeding: apply a tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound if trained to do so.
7. Do not remove embedded objects — stabilize them in place.

BURNS
Minor burn (redness, no blisters):
1. Cool the burn with cool (not cold/ice) running water for 10-20 minutes.
2. Cover with a sterile bandage or clean cloth.
3. Do not apply butter, oil, toothpaste, or ice.
4. Take pain reliever like paracetamol if needed.
5. Seek medical attention if burn is on face, hands, feet, genitals, or over a joint.

Severe burn (large area, deep, white/charred skin, blisters):
Call 108/911 immediately. Do not remove burned clothing. Do not cool a large burn with water (risk of shock). Cover loosely with a clean sterile dressing.

SEIZURE
1. Stay calm. Do not put anything in the person's mouth.
2. Clear the area of hard or sharp objects.
3. Place something soft under the head.
4. Turn the person gently on their side (recovery position) to prevent choking.
5. Time the seizure.
6. Call 108/911 if: the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, the person doesn't regain consciousness, another seizure follows, the person is pregnant, injured, or has diabetes.
7. Stay with the person until fully conscious.

STROKE — F.A.S.T. METHOD
F — Face drooping (ask them to smile — is it uneven?)
A — Arm weakness (ask them to raise both arms — does one drift down?)
S — Speech difficulty (ask them to repeat a phrase — is it slurred?)
T — Time to call 108/911 IMMEDIATELY. Time is critical in stroke treatment.

Other stroke symptoms: sudden numbness on one side, sudden vision change, sudden severe headache with no cause, sudden loss of balance or coordination.

HEART ATTACK SYMPTOMS
- Chest pain, pressure, tightness, squeezing (may radiate to arm, jaw, back, neck)
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness
Women may have atypical symptoms: unusual fatigue, jaw/neck/back pain, indigestion.
Action: Call 108/911 immediately. Have the person sit or lie comfortably. Loosen tight clothing. If not allergic, give 300mg aspirin (chew it). Begin CPR if they lose consciousness and stop breathing.

ANAPHYLAXIS (SEVERE ALLERGIC REACTION)
Symptoms: hives, swelling of face/throat, difficulty breathing, rapid heartbeat, dizziness, vomiting.
1. Call 108/911 immediately.
2. Use epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available — inject into outer thigh.
3. Have the person lie down with legs elevated (unless breathing is difficult, then sit up).
4. A second EpiPen dose may be given after 5-15 minutes if symptoms do not improve.
5. Monitor breathing. Begin CPR if they stop breathing.

FAINTING
1. Lay the person flat on their back.
2. Elevate legs 8-12 inches if possible.
3. Loosen any tight clothing.
4. Do not give water if unconscious.
5. If they do not regain consciousness within 1 minute, call 108/911.
6. Check for breathing and pulse. Begin CPR if absent.

FRACTURES AND SPRAINS
For suspected fracture: Immobilize the injured area. Do not try to straighten it. Apply ice wrapped in cloth (not directly on skin). Elevate if possible. Seek emergency care. Call 108/911 for severe fractures or spine/neck injuries.

NOSEBLEED
1. Sit upright and lean slightly forward (not back — swallowing blood causes nausea).
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose just below the bony bridge.
3. Hold for 10-15 minutes without checking.
4. Breathe through the mouth.
5. Apply a cold pack to the bridge of the nose.
6. Seek care if bleeding doesn't stop after 20 minutes or is very heavy.

HEAT STROKE
Symptoms: body temperature above 40°C (104°F), absence of sweating (dry skin), rapid heartbeat, confusion, loss of consciousness.
1. Call 108/911 immediately — this is a medical emergency.
2. Move to a cool place immediately.
3. Cool the person rapidly (ice packs to armpits, groin, neck; cool water on skin; fan).
4. Do not give fluids to someone unconscious.

HYPOGLYCEMIA (LOW BLOOD SUGAR)
Symptoms in diabetics: shakiness, sweating, rapid heartbeat, hunger, confusion, pale skin.
Rule of 15: Eat 15g fast carbohydrates (glucose tablets, half cup juice, 3-4 teaspoons of sugar). Wait 15 minutes. Recheck. Repeat if still low. If unconscious, call 108/911 — do not give food or drink to unconscious person.

POISONING OR OVERDOSE
Call 108 (India) or Poison Control (USA: 1-800-222-1222) immediately.
Do not induce vomiting unless instructed by medical personnel.
Try to identify the substance taken and provide this information to emergency services.
