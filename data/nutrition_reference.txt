NUTRITION AND HEALTHY EATING — MEDICAL REFERENCE

MACRONUTRIENTS

CARBOHYDRATES
The body's primary energy source. Found in grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes, and dairy. Simple carbohydrates (sugars) are digested quickly. Complex carbohydrates (fiber and starch) are digested slowly and provide sustained energy.
Recommended intake: 45-65% of total daily calories.
Healthy sources: whole grains (oats, brown rice, quinoa), legumes (lentils, chickpeas, beans), vegetables, fruits, sweet potatoes.
Limit: white bread, white rice, pastries, sugary drinks, candy, processed foods.

PROTEINS
Essential for building and repairing tissues, making enzymes and hormones, and supporting the immune system. Made from amino acids — 9 are essential (must come from diet).
Recommended intake: 10-35% of total daily calories. General guideline: 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight. Athletes may need 1.2-2.0 grams per kilogram.
Healthy sources: lean meats (chicken, turkey), fish (salmon, tuna, sardines), eggs, dairy (yogurt, cottage cheese), legumes, tofu, tempeh, nuts, seeds.
Complete proteins (contain all essential amino acids): meat, fish, eggs, dairy, soy.
Incomplete proteins (combine for complete): beans + rice, peanut butter + whole wheat.

FATS
Essential for cell membrane structure, hormone production, absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), and brain function. Not all fats are created equal.
Recommended intake: 20-35% of total daily calories.
Healthy fats (monounsaturated and polyunsaturated): olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds, fatty fish, flaxseed.
Saturated fats (limit): butter, lard, full-fat dairy, fatty red meat.
Trans fats (avoid): partially hydrogenated oils found in some processed foods, margarine, fast food.
Omega-3 fatty acids (healthy, anti-inflammatory): fatty fish (salmon, mackerel, sardines), walnuts, flaxseeds, chia seeds.

FIBER
A type of carbohydrate the body cannot digest. Supports digestive health, lowers cholesterol, stabilizes blood sugar, and promotes fullness.
Recommended intake: 25-38 grams per day.
Soluble fiber (dissolves in water, lowers cholesterol): oats, apples, citrus, beans, psyllium.
Insoluble fiber (adds bulk, prevents constipation): whole wheat, vegetables, nuts, seeds.
High-fiber foods: lentils (16g/cup), black beans (15g/cup), split peas (16g/cup), oats (4g/cup), broccoli (5g/cup), berries (4-8g/cup), almonds (3.5g/28g).

MICRONUTRIENTS

VITAMINS
Vitamin A: vision, immune function, skin health. Sources: liver, egg yolks, dairy, orange/yellow vegetables (carrots, sweet potatoes), leafy greens.
Vitamin B12: nerve function, red blood cells, DNA synthesis. Sources: meat, fish, eggs, dairy. Vegans require supplementation.
Vitamin B9 (Folate/Folic Acid): cell division, critical in pregnancy for neural tube development. Sources: leafy greens, legumes, fortified foods. Supplement before and during pregnancy.
Vitamin C: antioxidant, immune function, collagen synthesis, iron absorption. Sources: citrus fruits, bell peppers, strawberries, kiwi, broccoli.
Vitamin D: bone health, immune function, mood regulation. Produced by skin with sun exposure. Sources: fatty fish, egg yolks, fortified foods. Supplement often needed in areas with limited sunlight.
Vitamin E: antioxidant, immune function, skin health. Sources: nuts, seeds, vegetable oils, spinach.
Vitamin K: blood clotting, bone health. Sources: leafy greens (kale, spinach), broccoli, Brussels sprouts.

MINERALS
Calcium: bone and teeth health, muscle and nerve function. Sources: dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens, sardines with bones, tofu. Adults need 1000-1200mg daily.
Iron: oxygen transport in blood (hemoglobin), energy metabolism. Sources: red meat, poultry, fish, beans, lentils, fortified cereals, tofu, spinach. Iron from plant sources absorbed better with vitamin C.
Magnesium: muscle and nerve function, blood sugar regulation, bone development, energy production. Sources: nuts, seeds, legumes, whole grains, leafy greens, dark chocolate.
Potassium: blood pressure regulation, heart function, fluid balance. Sources: bananas, potatoes, sweet potatoes, avocados, spinach, beans, tomatoes.
Zinc: immune function, wound healing, taste and smell, DNA synthesis. Sources: meat, shellfish, legumes, nuts, seeds, dairy.
Sodium: fluid balance, nerve and muscle function. Most people consume too much. Limit sodium to less than 2300mg per day. Less than 1500mg if you have hypertension.

SPECIAL DIETS

DASH DIET (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)
Designed to lower blood pressure. Emphasizes fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins, and low-fat dairy. Limits sodium, red meat, sweets, and saturated fats. Very effective for lowering blood pressure.

MEDITERRANEAN DIET
Rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and olive oil. Moderate fish and seafood. Limited dairy and red meat. Associated with reduced risk of heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and cognitive decline.

LOW GLYCEMIC INDEX DIET
For blood sugar management (diabetes, prediabetes, PCOS). Focuses on foods that raise blood sugar slowly. Low GI foods: non-starchy vegetables, legumes, whole grains, most fruits.

ANTI-INFLAMMATORY DIET
To reduce chronic inflammation linked to heart disease, arthritis, cancer. Includes: fatty fish, colorful fruits and vegetables, nuts, olive oil, turmeric, ginger, green tea. Avoids: processed foods, refined sugar, trans fats, excess alcohol, refined carbohydrates.

VEGETARIAN AND VEGAN DIETS
Can be healthy when well-planned. Key nutrients to monitor: protein, iron, calcium, zinc, iodine, omega-3, vitamin B12 (must supplement), vitamin D. Plant-based protein sources: tofu, tempeh, edamame, lentils, chickpeas, black beans, quinoa (complete protein), nutritional yeast.

NUTRITION DURING PREGNANCY
Increased needs: folic acid (600mcg/day), iron (27mg/day), calcium (1000mg/day), iodine, choline, omega-3 (DHA).
Foods to avoid: raw or undercooked meat, fish high in mercury (shark, swordfish, king mackerel), unpasteurized cheese, raw eggs, alcohol, high-caffeine drinks.

NUTRITION FOR CHILDREN
Prioritize: calcium for bone development, iron for brain development, zinc for growth, omega-3 for brain and eye development, vitamin D for bone health.

HYDRATION
The human body is 60% water. Water supports all bodily functions. Recommended intake: about 2-3 liters per day for adults. Needs increase with exercise, heat, pregnancy, breastfeeding, and illness. Signs of dehydration: dark yellow urine, dry mouth, headache, fatigue, dizziness.
Best beverages: water, herbal teas, milk. Limit: sugary drinks, energy drinks, excessive fruit juice, excessive caffeine.

WEIGHT MANAGEMENT
Healthy weight is associated with lower risk of chronic disease. BMI (Body Mass Index) is a screening tool: Underweight < 18.5, Normal 18.5-24.9, Overweight 25-29.9, Obese >= 30.
For weight loss: create a moderate calorie deficit (500 calories/day = approximately 0.5kg per week loss). Combine dietary changes with regular physical activity. Focus on sustainable habits, not crash diets.
