COMMON MEDICATIONS — REFERENCE GUIDE

IMPORTANT: This reference is for educational purposes only. Always consult a licensed pharmacist or doctor before taking any medication, changing doses, or combining medications.

PAIN RELIEVERS (ANALGESICS)

PARACETAMOL (ACETAMINOPHEN / TYLENOL / CROCIN / DOLO 650)
Used for: Mild to moderate pain, fever (antipyretic). Headaches, toothaches, back pain, menstrual cramps, arthritis.
How it works: Reduces fever by affecting the heat-regulating center in the brain. Relieves pain by blocking pain signals.
Adult dose: 500-1000mg every 4-6 hours. Maximum 4000mg per day (less in liver disease or heavy alcohol use).
Side effects: Generally well-tolerated. Overdose can cause serious liver damage. Do not exceed recommended dose.
Warning: Do not combine with alcohol. Check other medicines for paracetamol content (many cold/flu products contain it).

IBUPROFEN (ADVIL, NUROFEN, BRUFEN)
Used for: Pain, fever, inflammation. Headaches, dental pain, menstrual cramps, muscle aches, arthritis, minor injuries.
How it works: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). Blocks prostaglandins that cause pain, fever, and inflammation.
Adult dose: 200-400mg every 4-6 hours. Maximum 1200mg per day (OTC). Up to 3200mg per day under medical supervision.
Side effects: Stomach upset, nausea, heartburn. Take with food or milk. Long-term use can cause stomach ulcers, kidney problems.
Warning: Avoid with kidney disease, heart failure, peptic ulcer, pregnancy (third trimester), or if on blood thinners. Do not give to children under 6 months without doctor advice.

ASPIRIN (ACETYLSALICYLIC ACID)
Used for: Pain, fever, inflammation. Also used at low doses (75-100mg) to prevent heart attack and stroke (antiplatelet effect).
Side effects: Stomach irritation, bleeding risk, Reye's syndrome (do not give to children under 16 with viral illness).
Warning: Increases bleeding risk. Avoid with blood thinners, kidney disease, or before surgery.

NAPROXEN (ALEVE, NAPROSYN)
Used for: Pain and inflammation. Longer-acting than ibuprofen.
Adult dose: 500mg twice daily.
Warning: Same NSAID cautions as ibuprofen — avoid with stomach ulcers, kidney disease, heart disease.

ANTIBIOTICS

IMPORTANT: Antibiotics only work against bacterial infections. They do NOT work against viral infections (common cold, flu, COVID-19). Never take antibiotics without a doctor's prescription. Always complete the full course even if you feel better.

AMOXICILLIN
Used for: Ear infections, throat infections, sinus infections, urinary tract infections, skin infections, pneumonia, dental infections, H. pylori (with other drugs).
Family: Penicillin antibiotic.
Common side effects: Diarrhea, nausea, skin rash. Serious: allergic reactions (can be severe).
Warning: Tell doctor about penicillin allergy before taking.

AZITHROMYCIN (ZITHROMAX / Z-PAK)
Used for: Chest infections, pneumonia, tonsillitis, ear infections, sexually transmitted infections (chlamydia).
Common side effects: Nausea, diarrhea, stomach pain.
Warning: May affect heart rhythm (QT prolongation). Discuss with doctor if you have heart problems.

CIPROFLOXACIN
Used for: Urinary tract infections, skin infections, respiratory infections, traveler's diarrhea, anthrax exposure.
Family: Fluoroquinolone antibiotic.
Warning: Do not use with antacids containing magnesium or aluminum (take 2 hours apart). Can cause tendon problems, especially in older adults. Avoid in children and pregnancy.

METRONIDAZOLE (FLAGYL)
Used for: Bacterial vaginal infections, dental infections, abdominal infections, parasite infections (giardia, amoeba), H. pylori.
Warning: Do not drink alcohol during treatment and for 48 hours after — causes severe nausea and vomiting. Can cause metallic taste.

DOXYCYCLINE
Used for: Chest infections, acne, Lyme disease, malaria prevention, STI chlamydia.
Warning: Take with plenty of water. Do not lie down for 30 minutes after taking. Avoid with dairy, antacids, or iron supplements (take 2 hours apart). Avoid in pregnancy and children under 8.

DIABETES MEDICATIONS

METFORMIN
Used for: First-line treatment for type 2 diabetes. May also be used for prediabetes and PCOS.
How it works: Reduces glucose production in the liver and improves insulin sensitivity.
Common side effects: Nausea, diarrhea, stomach upset (usually improve over time). Take with food.
Serious side effect: Lactic acidosis (very rare). Temporarily stopped before contrast imaging procedures.
Warning: Not for severe kidney disease. Avoid excessive alcohol.

GLIPIZIDE / GLIMEPIRIDE / GLICLAZIDE (SULFONYLUREAS)
Used for: Type 2 diabetes.
How they work: Stimulate the pancreas to release more insulin.
Serious side effect: Hypoglycemia (low blood sugar) — can be serious. Always carry sugar (glucose tablets, candy) with you.

BLOOD PRESSURE MEDICATIONS

LISINOPRIL / ENALAPRIL / RAMIPRIL (ACE INHIBITORS)
Used for: High blood pressure, heart failure, after heart attack, diabetic kidney disease.
Common side effects: Dry persistent cough (most common), dizziness (especially first dose).
Warning: Do not use in pregnancy. Monitor potassium levels. Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen).

LOSARTAN / VALSARTAN / TELMISARTAN (ARBs - Angiotensin Receptor Blockers)
Used for: High blood pressure, heart failure, kidney protection in diabetes.
Side effects: Dizziness, elevated potassium. Less likely to cause cough than ACE inhibitors.
Warning: Do not use in pregnancy.

AMLODIPINE / NIFEDIPINE (CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS)
Used for: High blood pressure, angina (chest pain).
Common side effects: Ankle swelling, facial flushing, headache.

CHOLESTEROL MEDICATIONS

ATORVASTATIN / ROSUVASTATIN / SIMVASTATIN (STATINS)
Used for: Lower LDL (bad) cholesterol, reduce heart attack and stroke risk.
Take: Usually taken once daily in the evening. Can be taken with or without food.
Common side effects: Muscle aches and pains (myalgia). Rarely, serious muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis).
Warning: Avoid grapefruit and grapefruit juice with some statins. Avoid in pregnancy and liver disease.

MENTAL HEALTH MEDICATIONS

SSRIs (SELECTIVE SEROTONIN REUPTAKE INHIBITORS)
Examples: Fluoxetine (Prozac), Sertraline (Zoloft), Escitalopram (Lexapro), Paroxetine (Paxil), Citalopram (Celexa).
Used for: Depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, PTSD, panic disorder.
How they work: Increase serotonin availability in the brain.
Side effects: Nausea, insomnia, sexual dysfunction, weight change (usually subside within weeks).
Important: Do not stop suddenly — taper under doctor supervision. May take 2-6 weeks to see full benefit. Rarely may increase suicidal thoughts in young people — monitor closely when starting.

OMEPRAZOLE / PANTOPRAZOLE / RABEPRAZOLE (PROTON PUMP INHIBITORS)
Used for: Acid reflux, GERD, stomach ulcers, H. pylori treatment.
Common side effects: Headache, nausea, diarrhea. Long-term use may affect magnesium and vitamin B12 absorption.
Best taken: 30 minutes before meals.

ANTIHISTAMINES
CETIRIZINE (ZYRTEC), LORATADINE (CLARITIN), FEXOFENADINE (ALLEGRA)
Used for: Allergies, hay fever, hives. Non-drowsy second-generation antihistamines.
DIPHENHYDRAMINE (BENADRYL): First-generation antihistamine. Effective but causes significant drowsiness. Used for allergies, sleep aid, motion sickness.

DRUG INTERACTIONS — KEY WARNINGS
- Ibuprofen + Blood thinners (warfarin, aspirin): increased bleeding risk
- Metformin + Alcohol: increased lactic acidosis risk
- Statins + Grapefruit juice: increased drug levels and side effect risk
- SSRIs + Tramadol/Triptans: serotonin syndrome risk
- Ciprofloxacin + Antacids: reduced antibiotic absorption
- ACE inhibitors + NSAIDs: kidney damage risk
- Warfarin + Many medications: always disclose all medicines to your doctor
