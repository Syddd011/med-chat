MENTAL HEALTH — COMPREHENSIVE MEDICAL REFERENCE

DEPRESSION (MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER)

DEFINITION
Depression is a common and serious mental health condition that negatively affects how you feel, think, and handle daily activities. It involves persistent feelings of sadness, hopelessness, and loss of interest in activities once enjoyed.

SYMPTOMS (must persist for at least 2 weeks)
- Persistent sad, anxious, or "empty" mood
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities
- Feelings of hopelessness, worthlessness, or guilt
- Decreased energy and fatigue
- Difficulty concentrating, remembering, or making decisions
- Sleep disturbances (insomnia or oversleeping)
- Appetite or weight changes
- Thoughts of death or suicide
- Physical symptoms like headaches, digestive problems with no clear cause

TYPES OF DEPRESSION
- Major depressive disorder
- Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia) — chronic, milder depression lasting 2+ years
- Seasonal affective disorder (SAD)
- Postpartum depression — after giving birth
- Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD)
- Bipolar disorder (includes depressive episodes)

CAUSES
Combination of genetic, biological, environmental, and psychological factors including brain chemistry imbalance, hormonal changes, family history, personal history of trauma or abuse, certain medications, chronic illness, substance abuse.

TREATMENT
- Psychotherapy: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Interpersonal Therapy (IPT), Behavioral Activation
- Medications: SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram), SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine), TCAs, MAOIs
- Combination of therapy and medication is often most effective
- Lifestyle: Regular exercise, adequate sleep, healthy diet, social connection, stress management
- ECT (electroconvulsive therapy) for severe treatment-resistant cases

ANXIETY DISORDERS

TYPES
- Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD): excessive, uncontrollable worry about everyday things
- Panic Disorder: repeated panic attacks with physical symptoms
- Social Anxiety Disorder: intense fear of social situations
- Specific Phobias: intense fear of specific objects or situations
- Agoraphobia: fear of places where escape feels difficult
- Separation Anxiety Disorder

SYMPTOMS OF ANXIETY
- Excessive worry or fear
- Restlessness, feeling edge
- Fatigue
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability
- Muscle tension
- Sleep problems

PANIC ATTACK SYMPTOMS
A panic attack is a sudden, intense episode of fear that triggers severe physical reactions including: rapid heartbeat, shortness of breath, chest pain, dizziness, nausea, trembling, sweating, chills, numbness, feeling of unreality, fear of losing control or dying. A panic attack is not dangerous but feels very frightening.

TREATMENT FOR ANXIETY
- Psychotherapy: CBT is most effective, Exposure therapy, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy
- Medications: SSRIs, SNRIs, Buspirone, Benzodiazepines (short-term only), Beta-blockers
- Lifestyle: Regular exercise, limiting caffeine and alcohol, good sleep, relaxation techniques, mindfulness, yoga

POST-TRAUMATIC STRESS DISORDER (PTSD)

DEFINITION
PTSD can develop after experiencing or witnessing a traumatic event such as war, natural disaster, accident, assault, or abuse.

SYMPTOMS
- Flashbacks and intrusive memories
- Nightmares
- Emotional numbness
- Avoidance of trauma reminders
- Negative thoughts and feelings
- Hypervigilance — being constantly on alert
- Sleep disturbances
- Difficulty concentrating
- Angry outbursts

TREATMENT
- Trauma-focused CBT
- EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing)
- Medications: SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine are FDA-approved for PTSD)

OBSESSIVE-COMPULSIVE DISORDER (OCD)

Characterized by recurring, unwanted thoughts (obsessions) and/or repetitive behaviors (compulsions). OCD is treated with CBT (specifically Exposure and Response Prevention - ERP) and medications (SSRIs).

BIPOLAR DISORDER

Characterized by extreme mood swings including emotional highs (mania or hypomania) and lows (depression). Treated with mood stabilizers (lithium, valproate), atypical antipsychotics, and psychotherapy.

SELF-CARE AND COPING STRATEGIES
- Maintain a regular routine
- Exercise regularly — even 30 minutes of walking helps
- Get enough sleep (7-9 hours for adults)
- Connect with supportive friends and family
- Limit alcohol and avoid drugs
- Practice mindfulness, meditation, or deep breathing
- Journaling can help process emotions
- Join a support group
- Reduce stressors where possible

WHEN TO SEEK HELP
Seek professional mental health support if mental health symptoms affect your daily life, work, or relationships. In India, contact iCall: 9152987821 or Vandrevala Foundation: 1860-2662-345. In USA, call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline). In a mental health emergency, call 108 (India) or 911 (USA).

WARNING SIGNS OF MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS
- Talking about wanting to die or kill oneself
- Looking for ways to kill oneself
- Talking about being a burden to others
- Increasing use of alcohol or drugs
- Withdrawing from activities and isolation
- Extreme mood swings
If you or someone you know shows these signs, seek help immediately.
